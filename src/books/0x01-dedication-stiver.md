# Dedication

## In memory of Stiver

This book and this project are dedicated to the memory of the person known as Stiver — the creator of Flibusta.

He made more than a site with books. It was a cozy place and a community of ideas, where people talked, argued and shared knowledge, and where information was available to everyone. People did not just take books there: they wrote reviews, kept blogs, quarrelled about translations on the forum, proofread other people's files and fixed their typos. The library was the occasion; what held it together were people who cared. It is a great contribution to culture, and it deserves to be remembered.

I learned of his death in 2024. I did not know him and cannot say anything about him that any reader of his library would not know. So there will be no biography here: writing about a person from hearsay is a poor way to remember him. I can speak only of what I saw myself: of what was made.

And this is what was made. A site that lived for years under blocks, moves and load stayed fast and clear. Every book had a short address with a number — `/b/N` — and that address never changed. The whole catalog could be taken away: database dumps, daily archives, OPDS for e-readers. Access did not depend on a single door: mirrors, onion, I2P, alternative DNS. None of this is accident or luck; these are engineering decisions, and behind each one is a single thought: the library must survive trouble, and the reader must reach the book.

In 2026 I saw a painting of a sailing ship at the home of someone close to me. It reminded me of a project that stays afloat to this day — and I decided to make Clipper after all. The painting became the logo, and the ship gave the name: clippers were the fastest sailing ships.

### What "dedicated" means

A dedication is not a sign over the door. It commits to a few things, and they can be used to check whether it has been kept.

First: a place to talk matters more than a shelf. That is why Clipper had, from the very start, not only books but comments, forums, anonymous boards, drafts, quotes and member pages. Without them the site would be a file server.

Second: an address is a promise. A number once given to a book, a person or a quote never changes and is never reused. In the code this is guarded by separate counters that survive a catalog rebuild.

Third: the site must be cheap to run. A library that needs a cluster lives as long as someone pays for the cluster. Clipper is one executable with no dependencies, started with one command, working the same on a new laptop and on a twenty-year-old machine.

Fourth: nothing may be hidden. The catalog is in an open format, members' records are in a text journal readable by eye, and a book goes to the reader as txt, fb2 and epub. Whoever wants to take their data or set up a mirror should not have to ask anyone's permission.

Clipper is not Flibusta and not its continuation. It is an attempt to understand how a well-made thing works and to make one's own with the same attitude to the reader. If this book helps someone build a place where people talk about books, the dedication is fulfilled.
